Three ways to use it

A practical path from launch to ongoing visibility.

Each level keeps the website and every third-party account under the customer’s control. The difference is who performs and maintains the work.

01

Included launch baseline

Accountless essentials

For public sites we build, the launch baseline covers the supported technical work that does not require a third-party account.

  • Indexability, canonical URL, title and description review
  • Robots and XML sitemap generation and validation
  • Relevant structured data and share metadata
  • Google, Bing and major AI-crawler access review
  • IndexNow notification where it is supported and appropriate

What it does: removes avoidable technical barriers. What it cannot do: force an engine to crawl, cite, index, or rank a page.

02

Self-service setup

  • A clean handoff checklist for the customer or marketing team

Best for: teams comfortable following a checklist and keeping their own accounts healthy.

AI discovery reality check

There is no universal “submit my business to AI” form.

ChatGPT search, Perplexity, and other answer engines discover and cite public material through their own crawling, search partnerships, indexes, and product rules. QueryKind improves the source website: crawl access, clear entity information, structured facts, useful pages, and references worth citing.

That can improve eligibility and clarity. It cannot guarantee inclusion, wording, placement, traffic, leads, or revenue—and we will never report those outcomes without evidence.

Built-in self-service playbook

Set up the parts that require ownership.

Interfaces change. Use these as durable guardrails, then follow the current prompts in each provider. Keep the business as primary owner.

Google Search ConsoleCustomer-owned search property
  1. Sign in with a business-controlled Google account.
  2. Add a Domain property when DNS access is available; otherwise use the exact URL-prefix property.
  3. Complete the provider’s ownership verification. DNS changes should be made only by an authorized domain administrator.
  4. Submit the public sitemap URL and review indexing, security, enhancement, and manual-action reports.
  5. For managed service, invite the named operator with the least role needed. Keep the customer as verified owner.
Bing Webmaster ToolsCustomer-owned search property
  1. Sign in with a business-controlled account and add the exact site.
  2. Verify ownership directly or import a verified Search Console property if the current Bing flow offers it and the customer approves.
  3. Submit the sitemap and confirm IndexNow status when the site uses it.
  4. Review crawl, index and site-scan reports; do not interpret submission as guaranteed indexing.
  5. Delegate access rather than sharing the primary login.
ChatGPT search & PerplexityPublic-site readiness, not enrollment
  1. Keep important facts on public, crawlable pages with stable URLs.
  2. Use consistent business names, locations, services, authorship and update dates where they are real and relevant.
  3. Allow the search and answer crawlers the business actually wants; document intentional blocks separately from accidental ones.
  4. Publish original, useful material that can be independently checked and cited.
  5. Test representative questions periodically, recording the date, query, answer and sources without treating a single result as a ranking guarantee.
Google Business ProfileEligible local businesses only
  1. Create or claim the profile with a business-controlled Google account.
  2. Use the real operating name, category, service area, hours and contact details; do not add keywords to the name.
  3. Complete Google’s current verification method with an authorized business representative.
  4. Add the website and keep profile details consistent with the site.
  5. Invite managers when needed; never transfer primary ownership merely for routine website work.
Analytics & adsOptional measurement, separate from discovery
  1. Create analytics or advertising properties inside customer-owned accounts.
  2. Share only the public measurement or destination IDs through the site’s validated settings—not passwords or secret keys.
  3. Enable only the providers the business needs and publish accurate privacy choices.
  4. Load provider scripts only after the applicable consent choice; keep marketing off when consent is absent.
  5. Trigger lead or purchase measurements only after a verified successful action, never from raw form fields or a preview.

Managed access policy

Your accounts stay yours.

We use

Delegated access

Provider invitations, scoped roles, OAuth grants, or other supported user-access methods tied to a named operator.

We do not request

Shared secrets

No passwords, recovery codes, backup codes, session cookies, primary inbox access, or permanent transfer of ownership.

When work ends

Clean offboarding

Remove our access, preserve customer ownership, and provide a plain-language record of connected properties and active settings.

Some providers expose public browser identifiers—such as analytics measurement IDs—but private credentials still belong in protected provider or hosting systems, never in chat, source code, or a public settings page.
